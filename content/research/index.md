## Research Areas

<div class="research-areas-list">

### Neuromorphic & Brain-Inspired Computing
Biological brains achieve unmatched cognitive efficiency through asynchronous, event-driven signaling. We explore hardware architectures that mirror these dynamics, interfacing directly with bio-inspired sensors such as Dynamic Vision Sensors (DVS) and event-based auditory transducers. Key focus areas include low-latency event routing, on-chip synaptic plasticity, and spiking neural network (SNN) silicon implementations.

### Digital & Mixed-Signal IC Design
While digital circuits provide scaling and programmability, analog dynamics offer instantaneous physical computation. We design mixed-signal blocks that merge continuous-time analog signal processing with robust digital control, including adaptive winner-take-all (WTA) selector networks, low-power current-mode accumulators, and robust data converters for near-sensor processing.

### Sparse AI Hardware
Modern deep learning workloads are characterized by high computational redundancy and dynamic sparsity. We design dedicated silicon accelerators and compute-in-memory (CIM) architectures that exploit structured and unstructured sparsity across activations and weights, bypassing unnecessary memory transfers and multiply-accumulate operations.

### Hardware–Algorithm Co-design
Silicon constraints must inform neural architecture design, and algorithm semantics must dictate processor dataflows. By co-optimizing network topologies, low-bit quantization, and memory-hierarchy layout, we achieve orders-of-magnitude energy and area improvements over general-purpose silicon.

</div>
