## Research Vision

My research is centered around bridging the efficiency gap between advanced artificial intelligence algorithms and energy-constrained silicon hardware. By exploring **hardware–algorithm co-design**, **neuromorphic event-driven computing**, and **digital/mixed-signal integrated circuits**, I seek to design high-throughput, low-power processing architectures for the next generation of cognitive edge systems.

---

## Research Areas

### 1. Digital & Mixed-Signal IC Design
Designing energy-efficient CMOS circuits that combine analog signal processing with digital control, focusing on adaptive winner-take-all circuits, low-noise front-ends, and robust mixed-signal building blocks.

### 2. Neuromorphic / Brain-Inspired Computing
Investigating event-driven asynchronous processing architectures tailored for dynamic vision sensors (DVS) and spiking neural networks (SNNs), optimizing latency and dynamic power consumption.

### 3. Sparse AI Hardware & Accelerators
Developing compute-in-memory (CIM) and digital acceleration engines that exploit sparsity in attention mechanisms, activations, and weight tensors in deep learning workloads.

### 4. Hardware–Algorithm Co-design
Co-optimizing neural network topologies, quantization schemes, and dataflows alongside physical hardware constraints to maximize silicon area efficiency and energy scalability.
